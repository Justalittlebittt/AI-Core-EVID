## Why test this information family?
A firm's newly disclosed share-repurchase authorization could convey management valuation beliefs or prospective buy-side demand, but the effect can be weak, anticipated, correlated with firm size, and confounded by market regime. **An announcement is not actual purchases**. A plan used for employee incentives is economically different from a capital cancellation; never treat all repurchase headlines as one uniform positive sign. Neither a prior price increase nor decline is an eligibility gate.

## Decision-time validity requirements
1. Universe: all **eligible A shares** as of each decision date, including companies **without** buyback announcements, as time-matched controls. Do not sample only favorable announcements, substitute missing issuers, or infer full market coverage from search-engine hits.
2. Event: first **public disclosure** of the specific new plan; distinguish initial proposal, board authorization, revisions, actual execution, progress reports and cessation. De-duplicate same event; distinguish employee-incentive vs cancellation purpose.
3. Timestamp: document date alone does **not** establish intraday release timestamp. Until release time is verifiable, assume the **next eligible session** is the earliest possible decision and use an executable later entry; do not claim a same-day pre-announcement fill.
4. Only pre-decision fields: declared minimum/maximum buyback amount, purpose, cap, contemporaneously published shares outstanding/financial context and source vintage. Do not use subsequently executed amount, later revisions, or future return to rank.
5. Target: fixed 20 exchange sessions of after-fee, after-slippage, realistically executable relative return, with matched issuer/market/sector controls and full universe/fill coverage. Predefine treatment, portfolio allocation, cash, delistings, risk, suspensions, daily price limits, board lot minimum and total-return baseline **before observing new qualifying outcomes**.
6. Evidence separation: one dated public document proves one example can be retrieved; it does not prove repeatable API access, historical coverage, independent OOS, actual-trade returns or a tradable alpha.
